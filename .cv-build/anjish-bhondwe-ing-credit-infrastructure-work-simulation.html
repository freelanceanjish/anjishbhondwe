5. Problem scenarios (real-time work)

Scenario A: Regulatory field must land in the Data Lake

Trigger: Policy update requires a new forbearance / DoD-related attribute before next reporting window.

Impact: PD / DoD-related engine cannot stay compliant without lake data.

Stakeholders: Credit Risk, Data Management, engineers, you (PO + journey).

Your move: Clarify acceptance criteria with Risk; cut backlog item spanning lake mapping + engine change; sequence lake first; track capacity; report risk to tribe if window slips.

Outcome: Field in lake, engine uses it, evidence ready for audit.

Scenario B: Broken facility–collateral join (LGD / LLP)

Trigger: Reconciliation shows collateral not linking to facilities for a product slice.

Impact: LGD wrong → LLP and capital noise.

Your move: Separate data defect vs engine bug; raise with Data Management + engineers; protect sprint capacity for the fix; keep Risk informed; document lineage for auditors.

Scenario C: Backlog conflict (policy vs tech debt vs model refresh)

Trigger: Three “P0” asks in one sprint: policy change, SAS job stabilisation, modeler feature for data mart.

Your move: Make trade-offs explicit on the backlog; regulatory deadline first if mandatory; park or slice tech debt; agree one modeler story that unblocks without flooding capacity.

Scenario D: Auditor asks for RWA-driving parameter evidence

Trigger: Internal audit wants lineage from source to engine output for a capital-relevant parameter.

Your move: Act as key contact; pull journey map (lake → engine → output); involve engineer for technical evidence; schedule walkthrough; backlog any control gap found.

Scenario E: Record Bank residual mapping

Trigger: Old Record Bank product codes still break a path in history or reporting join.

Your move: Treat as landscape expertise; map residual codes to ING taxonomy with Risk/Data; backlog a mapping fix; do not pretend day-one Record mastery, show how you ramp.

Scenario F: Digitalize a manual Risk spreadsheet

Trigger: Risk still reconciles engine outputs in Excel weekly.

Your move: Design a simple automated flow/tooling story; define acceptance with Risk; ship thin slice; measure time saved; feed digitalization narrative for SteerCo.

8. Hiring manager Q&A (domain interview)

Q1. What do you think Credit Infrastructure does?

A. It develops and maintains the engines that calculate credit-risk indicators and parameters that feed capital, RWA and LLP. It also keeps data infrastructure for future risk models and bridges Credit Risk, Data Management and operational teams. That matches the JD team description.

Q2. How do you see the 50/50 split in a normal week?

A. Roughly half on backlog, ceremonies, dependencies and stakeholder alignment. Half on landscape: PD/EAD/LGD journeys, lake regulatory work, flows and tooling. When a regulatory window hits, journey and PO work collapse into the same priority list.

Q3. Walk me through PD, EAD and LGD in this landscape.

A. They are core model-based outputs of the credit risk data landscape. Engines consume governed data (exposures, behaviour, collateral) and produce parameters that drive RWA and LLP. I have worked that chain at HSBC, Credit Suisse and on calculation engines at KBC. Credit risk is not a gap for me.

Q4. Give an example of owning an engine backlog under a regulatory deadline.

A. At KBC I owned and prioritised Basel IV and AnaCredit calculation engine backlog against NBB and ECB deadlines, keeping RWA, capital reporting and LLP-related windows on track with Risk, Data Management and IT.

Q5. How do you prioritise when Risk, Data Management and IT all say urgent?

A. Put trade-offs on the backlog in plain language: mandatory regulatory, capital/LLP impact, data defect blocking engines, then improvements. One conversation with the right people early. I did this as escalation point between risk, data and engineering at KBC and Credit Suisse.

Q6. What does Risk Data Journey mean to you here?

A. How credit risk data moves from need and sources through the Data Lake into PD/EAD/LGD and related engines, then out to capital, RWA, LLP and tooling for Risk and Business, with lineage auditors can follow.

Q7. How would you support a regulatory initiative in the Data Lake?

A. Clarify required fields and timing with Risk; align Data Management on landing and quality; sequence engine consumption after lake readiness; track delivery; keep audit trail. Same pattern as platform and regulatory programmes at KBC.

Q8. Oracle and SAS Enterprise Guide?

A. Hands-on from early banking roles (Standard Bank / Nihilent and HSBC) on risk reporting and credit risk data work. Later stacks differed, but Oracle and SAS are where I built foundation in regulated risk data. Ready to work in ING’s Credit Infrastructure stack.

Q9. Record Bank data landscape?

A. I know Record Bank was integrated into ING Belgium and residual credit data still matters in the map. I do not claim day-one Record table knowledge. I ramp the same way I learned multi-country landscapes at KBC: map sources, joins, and engine impact with the squad.

Q10. How do you work with auditors?

A. JD asks for key contact. At KBC I was escalation for audit-facing and SteerCo reporting on engine delivery health. I prepare lineage, reconciliations and clear status, and I backlog control gaps instead of defending them.

Q11. Your title says Director / CoE. Will you do hands-on PO work?

A. Yes. iostring was building delivery practice after KBC. I want long-term ownership again on one credit risk engine domain. This role is that: backlog, squad, landscape, auditors.

Q12. What would you digitalize first?

A. Whatever still forces Risk into manual reconciliations or spreadsheet controls around engine outputs. Thin automated flow or tooling slice with clear acceptance, then expand. That matches the JD digitalization duty.

Q13. How do you measure squad success here?

A. Regulatory windows met, engine stability, clear backlog, fewer data defects escaping to LLP/RWA noise, and audit questions answered without fire drill.

Q14. When can you join?

A. Returning Belgian resident path; docs ready. If offer and filing land in autumn, realistic join is December 2026 or January 2027 after re-entry process and return to Belgium.

Q15. Why ING Credit Infrastructure?

A. It sits exactly where I am strongest: credit risk engines, PD/EAD/LGD landscape, Risk–Data–IT bridge, and PO ownership under regulation. KBC is the closest twin; HSBC/CS add depth. Belgium is where I am returning.

9. Day-in-the-life Q&A (on the job)

Q16. Stand-up: engine batch failed overnight. What do you do?

A. Confirm impact with engineer (which parameters, which portfolios). If LLP/RWA path is at risk, notify Risk early. Decide: hotfix now vs planned story. Protect focus time; update board; avoid turning every incident into silent overtime.

Q17. Risk wants a change mid-sprint. Data Management says no capacity.

A. Make the constraint visible. If regulatory mandatory, escalate jointly and renegotiate sprint scope. If not, park on backlog with date. I do not promise both without capacity.

Q18. Engineer says the story is “data, not engine.” Risk says “engine is wrong.”

A. Journey expert hat: follow lineage one hop at a time (source → lake → engine input → calculation). Assign the fix to the right owner. Update acceptance criteria so the squad is not stuck in blame.

Q19. How do you write a good engine story?

A. Business/regulatory why; data inputs; engine behaviour change; acceptance including reconciliation or control check; dependency on lake/Data Management if any; audit evidence note if capital-relevant.

Q20. SteerCo asks for status in two sentences.

A. “Sprint focus is lake field X plus DoD/PD consumption for deadline Y. Risk: Data Management dependency; mitigation: joint refinement tomorrow and a cut scope if not ready by Wednesday.”

Q21. Chapter meeting vs squad work. What do you take where?

A. Squad: delivery and backlog. Chapter: craft (how POs/journey experts work, standards, coaching). I do not move capital deadlines into chapter theatre.

Q22. Modelers want ten new mart features. Squad has three slots.

A. Ask which feature unblocks model monitoring or next validation. Take one or two; sequence the rest. Keep engines stable if a policy deadline is live.

Q23. Business asks for a dashboard you do not own.

A. If it is Risk/Business tooling on engine outputs, shape a thin story. If it is portfolio reporting owned elsewhere, connect them to the reporting squad and keep your backlog clean.

Q24. You are new. How do you build landscape expertise fast?

A. Pair with one engineer and one Risk analyst. Draw the PD/EAD/LGD and related engine map. Sit in one batch failure and one lake change. Read one audit finding. Then own a small flow improvement so learning sticks.

Q25. Conflict: digitalization roadmap vs this month’s regulatory patch.

A. Patch and compliance first. Keep one digitalization slice alive if capacity allows so the roadmap does not die. Explain that openly to stakeholders.
