Question 1

What, in your opinion, is the single most critical success factor for a sustainable Agile transformation within a large organization?

Leaders who keep one prioritisation truth and hold their own decision discipline when pressure rises.

In a large IT organisation the coaches, ceremonies and tools move at the speed of management behaviour. When leaders protect a single backlog of organisational priorities, name who decides what, and stay with those choices under deadline heat, teams can improve maturity for real. Soft decision habits reopen every trade-off in side channels, and the same assessment and roadmap work returns every quarter.

So my first question in a transformation is always: who owns priority and decision rights on a bad week, and do those people still honour them? That is the success factor I watch.

Question 2

In a maximum of 2 pages, describe how you translate a transformation vision into a concrete roadmap, governance framework, leadership model, measurable objectives, and organizational buy-in. Illustrate with a real-world case study and your personal role.

My working method

I start on the floor. I talk to teams, coaches, architects, portfolio and managers and write down how work, escalation and governance actually run. Strengths, gaps and risks go into a short maturity picture everyone can argue with.

From there I agree a target operating model: roles, decision rights, and how the strategic and tactical layers connect to delivery. The roadmap follows that TOM. Each initiative gets an owner, a milestone, known dependencies and the management decision still open. Governance is the meeting cadence, escalation path and portfolio rule set that makes the TOM usable in IT. Objectives are a small KPI set on flow, predictability, quality and maturity, reported in a pack leaders can finish in a few minutes. Buy-in comes from coaching the Agile Coaching Team and coaching management in the same season, with methods left behind in the team.

Case: KBC Bank and Verzekering, Brussels and Leuven, 2021 to 2024

Setting. EMEA IT delivery across Belgium, Hungary, Czech Republic and Bulgaria, with onshore-offshore partners. Liquidity reporting, Kate Coin under ECB constraints and GDPR sat beside ordinary change. Capacity was shared and deadlines were real.

My seat. Delivery Lead. I coached the delivery vertical, ran capacity and priority arbitration, raised Agile maturity across squads and trains, and owned the SteerCo story with senior management. Product, Scrum Masters, architects, business and Risk were in the weekly mix.

Question 2 (continued)

Assessment. I mapped planning quality, impediment speed, backlog readiness and how leadership packs were used. Domain skill was high. Gaps showed up in uneven refinement, slow cross-country escalation and reports heavy on activity with thin outcome signal. I put those findings in front of management with a ranked improvement list.

Roadmap. With Product and management I sequenced the work: lock capacity and sprint-goal ownership, deepen coaching in the delivery vertical, then harden dependency handling between trains. Dates followed SteerCo and regulatory windows so the roadmap stayed tied to the calendar the organisation already respected.

Governance. We clarified intake ownership, who could cut scope inside a sprint, and how country dependencies escalated inside days. That gave teams a path and gave management a single place to decide.

Leadership. I worked with leaders on the habits that protect flow: one priority conversation, explicit trade-offs, and follow-through on retro actions. These were working sessions on live cases, including the tension between speed, quality and compliance.

Measures. SteerCo packs carried capacity versus demand, delivery risk and progress against committed outcomes. Flow and predictability numbers sat next to the narrative so improvement talks had a shared base.

Buy-in and my hand in it. People moved when they saw their own problems written clearly and saw the next concrete actions. I ran the assessment talks, wrote the improvement priorities, defended them in leadership forums, adjusted the operating rhythm, coached Scrum Masters and delivery partners, and kept the link between the vision and the week. That is the pattern I would bring to AWOW and EITS: assess, roadmap, TOM and governance, KPIs, strengthen the Coaching Team, and stay with management until decision discipline holds.
