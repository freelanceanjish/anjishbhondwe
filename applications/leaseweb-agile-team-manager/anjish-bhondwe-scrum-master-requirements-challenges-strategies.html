Every requirement mapped to real-time challenges and value-based ways of working
Anjish Bhondwe · Foundational Agile values (Manifesto) + Scrum values · Interview / day-1 reference

0. Foundational values (use these as your compass)

Agile Manifesto: Individuals and interactions over processes and tools · Working software over comprehensive documentation · Customer collaboration over contract negotiation · Responding to change over following a plan

Scrum values: CommitmentFocusOpennessRespectCourage

For every challenge below, the strategy should make one or more of these values visible in behaviour, not only in slides.

A. Key responsibilities

1. Facilitate Scrum ceremonies (Daily, Planning, Review, Retro)

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Stand-up becomes a status report to the SM or manager
  • Planning turns into estimation theatre; backlog not ready
  • Review is a demo of unfinished work or no stakeholder feedback
  • Retro is blame, silence, or actions that never get done
  • Meetings run long; people multitask; offshore join late or drop
  • Stand-up: team speaks to each other on progress toward the Sprint Goal; SM listens for impediments
  • Planning: protect Sprint Goal first, then capacity-based selection; timebox refinement upstream so Planning is decision, not discovery
  • Review: invite real users/stakeholders; capture feedback as backlog input the same day
  • Retro: one inspected problem, few concrete experiments, owner + check-in next Sprint
  • Rotate facilitators; keep agenda visible; agree remote etiquette (camera, mute, parking lot)
 Focus · Openness · Respect · Commitment · Interactions over process

2. Ensure proper adoption and application of Agile / Scrum

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • “Scrumfall”: Sprint labels on a waterfall plan
  • Skipping Definition of Done, Sprint Goal, or Product Goal
  • Managers still assign tasks person by person
  • Tool compliance mistaken for agility
  • Multiple frameworks mixed without agreement
  • Teach the “why” of each event/accountable role with short working agreements, not big training dumps
  • Make Sprint Goal and Definition of Done visible; inspect them every Review/Retro
  • Coach managers to ask for outcomes and impediments, not task lists
  • Use Jira to support transparency; do not let ticket hygiene replace conversation
  • Start with Scrum done well; add Kanban practices only where flow needs them (WIP limits, cycle time)
 Courage · Openness · Commitment · Responding to change · Working software

3. Support Product Owners with backlog management

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • PO is overloaded, absent, or proxy-only
  • Backlog is a dumping ground; no order, no sizing, vague stories
  • Stakeholders bypass PO and push work into the Sprint
  • Tech debt and quality never get ordered against features
  • Offshore team unclear on “ready” and acceptance
  • Protect PO time for refinement cadence; SM facilitates readiness, PO owns order and value
  • Agree a light Definition of Ready; split large items; clarify acceptance criteria before Planning
  • Route mid-Sprint requests through PO; make trade-offs explicit against Sprint Goal
  • Keep a visible quality/debt slice in the Product Goal conversation
  • Joint refinement with local + offshore; record decisions in the item, not only in chat
 Respect · Focus · Customer collaboration · Commitment · Openness

4. Monitor team capacity, velocity, and sprint progress

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Velocity used as a performance KPI or to compare teams
  • Capacity ignored (holidays, support load, onboarding)
  • Burndown gamed; work hidden outside the board
  • Scope added silently mid-Sprint
  • Metrics discussed only with management, not the team
  • Treat velocity as a forecasting aid for the same team over time, never as a target or ranking tool
  • Plan with real capacity (focus factor, support%, leave); revisit in Planning
  • Keep one board of truth; daily inspect Sprint Goal progress, not only story points left
  • Any scope change needs PO + team trade-off; update Sprint Goal if needed
  • Share charts in Review/Retro with the team first; ask “what does this tell us?”
 Openness · Focus · Respect · Commitment · Individuals & interactions

5. Remove impediments and protect teams from distractions

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • SM becomes a ticket dispatcher instead of an impediment remover
  • Escalations stuck in politics; no owner
  • “Quick asks” from leadership break focus every day
  • Environment, access, environments, licenses block delivery for days
  • Team learns learned helplessness: waits for SM to solve everything
  • Visible impediment board: owner, age, next action; escalate by impact on Sprint Goal
  • Coach the team to try first (within agreement), then escalate together
  • Create a focus agreement with stakeholders: intake path, quiet hours, who can interrupt
  • Partner with platform/ops early; track mean time to unblock as a health signal
  • Protect Sprint Goal publicly; say no or “not now” with a clear trade-off, not silence
 Courage · Focus · Servant leadership · Respect · Commitment

6. Coach teams, stakeholders, and business partners on Agile practices

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Stakeholders want certainty dates without backlog clarity
  • Business partners treat Sprints as mini-waterfalls with fixed scope + date
  • Team resists coaching (“we already know Scrum”)
  • Offshore and local have different maturity and habits
  • Coaching seen as process police
  • Coach with outcomes: forecasts as ranges, assumptions visible, Inspect & Adapt checkpoints
  • Run short stakeholder workshops: roles, Sprint Goal, Review purpose, how to give useful feedback
  • Use observation + questions before advice; teach in the moment during events
  • Align one working agreement across sites; pair facilitators; share retro themes across locations
  • Frame coaching as enabling delivery and reducing waste, not enforcing ceremony for its own sake
 Respect · Openness · Courage · Customer collaboration · Individuals & interactions

7. Promote continuous improvement and agile culture

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Retro actions die; same problems return
  • Fear of failure; no psychological safety
  • Improvement work never gets capacity
  • Culture is posters and slogans only
  • Local improvements never scale across teams
  • Limit WIP on improvements; put 1–2 experiments in the Sprint backlog with acceptance checks
  • Model blameless language; celebrate learning from failed experiments
  • Reserve a small, explicit improvement capacity each Sprint
  • Make culture visible in behaviours: how we handle conflict, feedback, and unfinished work
  • Share patterns in communities of practice; invite other teams to Reviews when useful
 Openness · Courage · Commitment · Respect · Responding to change

8. Support risk and issue management

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Risks live in a separate PMO register nobody looks at
  • Issues raised late; Sprint Goal already at risk
  • Delivery risk confused with people blame
  • Dependencies on other teams discovered mid-Sprint
  • Offshore timezone delays issue response
  • Keep risks/impediments next to the backlog and Sprint board; review in Daily and Planning
  • Early warning signals: aging WIP, blocked items, rising cycle time, unfinished Goal
  • Separate “what failed” from “who failed”; inspect system causes in Retro
  • Map dependencies in refinement; negotiate handoffs before Planning commits
  • Agree follow-the-sun escalation contacts and response SLAs between sites
 Openness · Courage · Focus · Commitment · Transparency

B. Required experience & skills

9. Minimum 3 years relevant Scrum Master experience

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Years counted, but depth is thin (ceremony-only SM)
  • Interview / role expects multi-team or org coaching beyond your past scope
  • Context switch into a new domain (banking, logistics, AI) with little runway
  • Show evidence of outcomes: flow, predictability, quality, engagement, not only “ran Scrums”
  • Be honest about scale; propose a 30–60–90 day listen-and-stabilize plan before big redesign
  • Learn domain language fast via PO pairing and stakeholder map; stay humble on content, strong on process
 Openness · Respect · Courage · Commitment

10. Strong knowledge of Agile methodologies (Scrum, Kanban)

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Team wants Scrum events but Kanban flow problems (queues, multitasking)
  • Push to “do SAFe / Spotify” without team readiness
  • Rigid purity debates (“that’s not Scrum”) blocking useful change
  • Use Scrum for complex product work; add Kanban for flow: WIP limits, classes of service, explicit policies
  • Fit framework to problem size; start with team-level empiricism before scaling theatre
  • Judge practices by Inspect & Adapt results, not textbook purity
 Focus · Responding to change · Working software · Openness

11. Experience with JIRA or similar Agile tools

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Over-configured workflows; team works around the tool
  • Dashboards for leadership diverge from team reality
  • Ticket spam; no conversation; offshore updates board only
  • Permissions and scheme chaos across projects
  • Keep workflow simple (To Do / Doing / Done + blocked); match Definition of Done in status meaning
  • One source of truth; same board used in Daily and Review
  • Tool supports transparency; decisions still happen in conversation
  • Partner with Jira admin; minimize custom fields; document a thin team convention
 Individuals & interactions over tools · Openness · Focus

12. Understanding of software development methodologies

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • SM cannot spot when “done” is risky (no review, no test, big-bang merge)
  • Devs dismiss SM as non-technical
  • Waterfall habits inside the Sprint (code all Sprint, test last day)
  • Learn enough of the delivery path to ask sharp questions without owning design
  • Earn respect by removing tech impediments and protecting quality time
  • Encourage thin vertical slices each Sprint; “done” means potentially releasable per Definition of Done
 Working software · Respect · Focus · Courage

13. Understanding of automated testing and CI/CD practices

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Manual testing bottleneck at Sprint end
  • Broken pipeline treated as “someone else’s problem”
  • Fear of release; rare deployments
  • SM pressed to commit dates while build is red
  • Make pipeline health a Sprint Goal risk; include test automation and fix-flaky-tests as backlog items
  • Definition of Done includes automated checks where the team agrees they are needed
  • Partner with tech leads on small, frequent integration; celebrate green builds and safe deploys
  • Never hide a red build to protect a date; make the trade-off visible (Courage + Openness)
 Working software · Courage · Openness · Commitment · Focus

14. Understanding of project management methodologies

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • PMO expects Gantt certainty, RAG status, and fixed scope
  • SM pulled into classic PM role (task assignment, reporting hero)
  • Hybrid programs: some waterfall vendors, some Agile teams
  • Translate: provide forecasts, risks, and outcome progress without fake precision
  • Stay in SM accountability: coach and facilitate; do not become the single point of delivery control
  • Agree integration points (milestones as learning points); keep team Sprint Goals intact inside the program
 Responding to change · Customer collaboration · Courage · Respect

15. Excellent communication and stakeholder management

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Mixed messages to team vs leadership
  • Stakeholders only hear bad news late
  • Conflict avoided until it explodes
  • Language/cultural noise with offshore partners
  • Same transparent story for all: Goal, progress, risks, asks
  • Cadence of Reviews + short written heartbeats; no surprise escalation
  • Facilitate conflict early with respect; separate interests from positions
  • Confirm understanding; prefer written decisions after verbal agreement across timezones
 Openness · Respect · Courage · Individuals & interactions

C. Soft skills

16. Servant leadership mindset

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Team expects the SM to decide and assign
  • Leadership equates servant leadership with “soft” or passive
  • SM over-serves and burns out; team never owns
  • Serve the team’s effectiveness: remove blocks, grow ownership, hold the process lightly but firmly
  • Be firm on Sprint Goal and Definition of Done; gentle on ego and blame
  • Ask “how can I help you succeed?” then step back so the team still owns the work
 Respect · Commitment · Focus · Courage

17. Strong coaching and facilitation abilities

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Facilitation becomes lecturing
  • Dominant voices own the room; quiet / offshore voices lost
  • Coaching advice ignored under delivery pressure
  • Design events so the team does the thinking; SM holds timebox and purpose
  • Use round-robin, silent writing, breakouts; call on remote first sometimes
  • Coach in small experiments tied to pain the team already feels
 Respect · Openness · Individuals & interactions · Focus

18. Proactive and solution-oriented approach

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Proactivity mistaken for taking over PO or tech lead work
  • Jumping to solutions before understanding the system
  • Optimism that hides real risk
  • Act early on impediments and clarity gaps; stay in SM lane
  • Diagnose with data and listening first; then propose options with trade-offs
  • Pair honesty with options: “Here is the risk, here are two paths”
 Courage · Openness · Focus · Commitment

19. Foster collaboration, trust, and continuous improvement

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Silos between dev, test, ops, business
  • Low trust after missed dates or blame culture
  • Improvement feels like extra meetings
  • Shared Sprint Goal and joint ownership of Done; cross-role pairing
  • Rebuild trust with small kept promises and transparent metrics
  • Fold improvement into existing events; measure if the pain reduced
 Respect · Openness · Commitment · Customer collaboration

20. Team-player mentality: knowledge sharing and empowerment

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Hero culture; knowledge in few heads
  • People withhold know-how for job security
  • Empowerment without clarity becomes chaos
  • Pairing, swarming, docs as part of Done; rotate module ownership
  • Reward teaching and backup coverage in feedback conversations
  • Empower within clear goals, Definition of Done, and working agreements
 Respect · Commitment · Openness · Courage

D. Language requirements

21. Fluent English with local and offshore teams

Real-time challengesAgile tackling strategiesValues
  • Accent, speed, or jargon leaves offshore unclear
  • Decisions made in hallway chats local-only
  • Written English in tickets is vague; rework follows
  • Timezones shrink overlap; handoffs fail
  • Speak plainly; check understanding; avoid idioms in critical decisions
  • Record decisions in backlog items / shared notes the same day
  • Definition of Ready includes clear acceptance language both sites can use
  • Design overlap windows for Planning/Refinement/Review; async updates for Daily where needed
 Respect · Openness · Individuals & interactions · Focus
How to use this in interview or first 90 days
  • Pick 2–3 requirements they care about most and tell a story: challenge you faced → value you held → strategy → result.
  • Do not recite the Manifesto. Show the value in the behaviour (“We protected Focus by…”, “Openness meant we showed the red build…”).
  • If they use hybrid / PMO language, translate without abandoning empiricism: forecast, inspect, adapt.
